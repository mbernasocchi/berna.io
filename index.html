01 A small beginning

A small project.
Three missing ECTS.

QField started as a small project during my geography degree, when I was three ECTS short. What came next was people joining in and making it useful to about half a million people every month.

It didn’t happen because I had a grand plan. QField grew because people joined in, shared what they knew and made it better. The same is true of OPENGIS.ch, which began with one person, and 27summits, which began over a glass of Merlot in a hut.

I like starting things, but I like what happens next even more: people taking an idea further than I could on my own.

03 Leading

Make room
for great people.

Good things grow when the people around them have space to grow too.

I lead a team of 45 at OPENGIS.ch, chair QGIS.org, a community of thousands of contributors and millions of users, and sit on the board of OSGeo. In all three places the job is much the same: listen first, look for the road everyone can walk on, and bring people with different views to the same table.

My favourite part is watching people take over things I used to do, and do them better. A team that can replace its boss is a healthy team, and it leaves me time to plant the next seed.

05 Outside the office

Every summit starts
at the lowest point.

I’ve reached the highest point of 26 Swiss cantons, starting from each canton’s lowest point and getting there under my own power. One summit to go.

In the mountains and in open source, I start small, go step by step and bring good people along. Ski touring, climbing or flying, it’s about curiosity as much as effort. With two small kids at home, the big days are rarer and the short ones count double.

06 A little context

The longer

Sports CV ↓

Marco Bernasocchi is a geographer, open-source advocate and the creator of QField, the field app for QGIS, which has passed two million downloads and is used by about half a million people every month. He has chaired QGIS.org since 2020 and has been on the board of the Open Source Geospatial Foundation since 2022.

In his day job he is CEO of OPENGIS.ch, which he founded in 2011 and which is now the second biggest contributor to QGIS core, with 45 people working on QGIS, QField, QFieldCloud and Georama. He speaks regularly about open-source sustainability, including FOSS4G keynotes in 2023 and 2025 and a talk at FOSDEM, and co-authored the reference paper on QGIS in Patterns.

He lives in Laax, a small Romansh-speaking village in the Swiss Alps, with his partner and their two kids, and spends as much time as he can in the mountains, ski touring, climbing or flying. He has reached the highest point of 26 Swiss cantons from their lowest point under his own power and still has one summit to go.

01 A small beginning

A small project.
Three missing credits.

QField started as a student project, when I was three credits short of my master’s degree in geography. I built the first versions on my own and then spent the next fifteen years finding people who could make it better than I ever could alone.

The same pattern runs through everything I have started. OPENGIS.ch began with one person and is now almost fifty. 27summits began over a glass of Merlot in a hut. None of it grew by accident. It grew because the right people came in and got the room to make it theirs.

I like starting things.
I like even more what happens when the right people take them further than I could.

03 Leading

Make room
for great people.

Good things grow when the people around them have space to grow too.

I lead a team of almost fifty at OPENGIS.ch, chair QGIS.org, a community of thousands of contributors and millions of users, and sit on the board of OSGeo. In all three places the job is much the same: listen first, look for the road everyone can walk on, and bring people with different views to the same table.

My favourite part is watching people take over things I used to do, and do them better. A team that can replace its boss is a healthy team, and it leaves me time to plant the next seed.

06 A little context

The longer
version.

Short CV ↓ Software CV ↓ Sports CV ↓

I am a geographer who ended up writing software, and an entrepreneur who ended up chairing a foundation. Both happened the same way: I started something small, went looking for people better than me, and gave them room.

The small thing was QField. I built the first version in 2011 as a Google Summer of Code project, porting QGIS to Android on my own, because I was a few credits short of my master’s degree and needed a project. Today a team at OPENGIS.ch builds it with a worldwide community, it has passed two million downloads, and more than half a million people use it every month to collect data in the field.

OPENGIS.ch is the company Matthias Kuhn and I founded. We are almost fifty people working on QGIS, QField, QFieldCloud and Georama, and the second biggest contributor to QGIS core. Since 2020 I have chaired QGIS.org, since 2022 I have sat on the board of the Open Source Geospatial Foundation.

I live in Laax, in a small Romansh-speaking village in the Swiss Alps, with my partner and our two kids. I speak Italian, German, French, English and Spanish, and I speak about open source as a business wherever a conference will have me. When I am not working, I am in the mountains: ski touring, climbing or flying, and slowly finishing 27summits, which has one summit left.
